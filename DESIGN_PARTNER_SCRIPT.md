# KH-01 design-partner conversation guide

## Proposed first customer

An engineering-led company with roughly 50–500 people and at least three active code repositories. Technical knowledge lives beside the code, while policies and onboarding knowledge live in scattered documents. The primary user is an operations, product, or engineering-adjacent writer; the likely buyer is the leader accountable for operations, product, or engineering effectiveness.

## Hypothesis to test

Teams will adopt an OKF knowledge hub if it lets non-technical authors publish company knowledge beside repository-owned knowledge without copying either into another proprietary silo.

## Who to recruit

Recruit three companies that meet all of these conditions:

- three or more active repositories with useful Markdown documentation;
- one person regularly translating technical context into company guidance;
- recurring uncertainty about which document is current or authoritative;
- willingness to walk through one real policy and three repository sources.

Avoid teams seeking an AI answer bot first; that is not the first-release job.

## 45-minute conversation

### 1. Current behaviour — 10 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time someone needed an answer that crossed product or operations documentation and a code repository.
2. Where did they look first? What happened next?
3. Who is allowed to update each source? How do you know an answer is current?
4. Show me one real example. Please use the actual tools and documents rather than describing an ideal process.

### 2. Cost and urgency — 10 minutes

1. How often does this happen?
2. What is the consequence of using an outdated answer?
3. Who feels that pain most, and who owns fixing it?
4. What have you already tried? Why did it fail or stall?

### 3. Workflow walkthrough — 15 minutes

Open the clickable narrative and ask the participant to drive.

1. Connect three repositories. Ask: “What would stop you connecting a real source here?”
2. Create the incident-communication policy. Ask: “Who would author, review, and publish this today?”
3. Search for incident communication. Ask: “Can you tell which answer is authoritative and why?”

Do not explain the interface unless the participant is blocked. Record where they hesitate and the words they use.

### 4. Commitment test — 10 minutes

1. Which real workflow would you trial first?
2. Who else must approve access to the repositories and company policies?
3. Would you run a two-week self-hosted pilot using real, non-sensitive content?
4. What would have to be true for you to keep using it after the pilot?

A compliment is not validation. A named owner, real content, and a scheduled follow-up are stronger signals.

## Proposed first-release success measure

At least two of three design partners can, in a 30-minute observed session and without Git or Markdown assistance:

1. connect three repository sources;
2. find one repository-owned concept and identify its source and freshness;
3. author and publish one company policy; and
4. retrieve both through one search.

Each successful partner must also agree to a two-week pilot with a named internal owner. This measure is proposed until the product owner confirms it.
